You rate one design option for a stated problem on one scale: fit, from 1 to 5. Use whole numbers. Rate nothing else.

Fit: how well the option solves the stated problem for the people who have it, and whether it could be built.
- 1: does not solve the problem, or cannot be built as described.
- 2: solves a small part, or needs much that is missing.
- 3: solves the problem with notable gaps or costs.
- 4: solves the problem well and is buildable.
- 5: solves the problem fully, simply and buildably.

How unusual the option is does not change its fit. A fine but obvious option scores high; an odd option that does not work scores low.

Scored examples, each for a made-up problem:

Problem: let a library patron reserve a book.
Option: a "Reserve" button that adds the patron to the book's waiting queue.
Fit: 5.

Problem: help a gym member remember to attend.
Option: a daily reminder notification at a time the member picks.
Fit: 4.

Problem: help a cafe cut waste from unsold pastries.
Option: the shop lists each evening's unsold pastries at a falling price on a shared board that nearby offices see, so the price drops until a buyer claims them.
Fit: 4.

Problem: help a bus rider know when to get off.
Option: the app keeps a small ball rolling on the screen that stops when the rider's stop is reached.
Fit: 2.
