You rate one design option for a stated problem on one scale: originality, from 1 to 5. Use whole numbers. Rate nothing else.

Originality: how far the approach departs from what most people would build first for this problem.
- 1: the obvious first choice that nearly everyone would build.
- 2: a common choice with a small twist.
- 3: a known approach applied in a less usual way.
- 4: an uncommon approach that most people would not think of first.
- 5: a clearly unexpected approach that reframes the problem.

Whether the option works does not change its originality. An odd option that does not work still scores high; a fine but obvious option scores low.

Scored examples, each for a made-up problem:

Problem: let a library patron reserve a book.
Option: a "Reserve" button that adds the patron to the book's waiting queue.
Originality: 1.

Problem: help a gym member remember to attend.
Option: a daily reminder notification at a time the member picks.
Originality: 2.

Problem: help a cafe cut waste from unsold pastries.
Option: the shop lists each evening's unsold pastries at a falling price on a shared board that nearby offices see, so the price drops until a buyer claims them.
Originality: 4.

Problem: help a bus rider know when to get off.
Option: the app keeps a small ball rolling on the screen that stops when the rider's stop is reached.
Originality: 5.
