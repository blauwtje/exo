# Marketing and document pages

A buyer judges the product, not the slogan, so a marketing page that shows the product doing its job fills the frame with signal. Copy on an empty ground is what makes these pages look blank.

## Hero

- Product working: a real-fidelity UI fragment built in HTML and CSS (a run timeline, a schedule, a leaderboard, an inbox) holding 15-40 specific data points: rows, names, statuses, amounts, timestamps. Not an illustration of a metaphor, not a blurred screenshot frame.
  - Good: a leaderboard with 20 named riders, times and gaps. Bad: a gradient blob behind a floating card.
- Layout: headline left and product right, or a short headline over the product full-bleed beneath it. A centred slogan with buttons on an empty hero is the generic SaaS template.
- Display: large (96-160px) and tightly tracked; the headline and the product together fill the first viewport, with no flat ground left over in either half.
- Asks: the primary call to action and the secondary link sit in the hero, beside the headline, not below the product.

## Sections

- Artifact per section: each section carries one artifact with its copy beside it: a product fragment, a flow diagram, a data table, a code or config block, a before and after. A section of copy alone becomes a band of text on an empty ground.
- Features: each feature shows its own fragment at its own size, the strongest one large; not a grid of identical icon cards.
- Steps: a numbered process shows what each step looks like in the product, not three icons with captions.
- Fill: a section's artifact and copy fill its band side to side at 1440; a narrow centred column inside a full-width band leaves two flat margins.

## Rhythm

- Bands: alternate in value or hue (deep, light, coloured), so the scroll has a beat. Equal pale bands merge into one blank field.
- Surfaces: no band is one flat value; a large band follows the large-ground rule in color.md.
- Density: follow the contract's density line; a tight page shortens gaps between sections, an airy one keeps them but still fills each band.

## Section patterns

- Proof: stats, logos and quotes only as the request supplies them; product data inside fragments is invented freely, proof never.
- Stats band: large numerals in the display face with their own units and a one-line label beside each, set on their own ground, not small numbers in a row of cards.
  - Good: "4.2 s" at 120px with "median cold start" beside it. Bad: four 24px numbers in white cards.
- Logo strip: wordmarks at real weight and size, each set the way that company would set its name, in one ink; not faint grey text.
- Quotes: the quote large, with name, role and company, placed beside the product moment it talks about.
- Pricing: the highlighted tier raised on its own ground or hue, with its price largest; the others plain and comparable line by line.
- FAQ: beside a product fragment or a contact panel, not a centred column of questions on an empty band.
- Code or terminal: a real command with real output lines, styled as the product's own surface, not a decorative window with three dots.
  - Good: `tidewrack sync --dry-run` with six output lines. Bad: a window with three dots around `npm install`.
- Closing call to action: its own band in the dominant hue, with a product detail or stat beside the action.
- Footer: link groups in columns at full width, with the product name and a short line, so the page ends designed rather than trailing off.

## Mobile

- 390: the hero product fragment stays, cropped or simplified to its most telling part, below the headline and asks; it is not dropped.
